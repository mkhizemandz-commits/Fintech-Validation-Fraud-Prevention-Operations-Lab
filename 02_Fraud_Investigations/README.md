# Fraud Investigation Cases

## Overview

This section demonstrates a structured approach to investigating potentially suspicious fintech applications.

The cases use fictional scenarios involving identity verification, document quality, phone numbers, devices, addresses, SIM changes, customer disputes, and potential system issues.

The purpose is to demonstrate investigation and decision-making skills rather than simply identifying applications as fraudulent.

**Important:** All cases are fictional. They do not represent PayJoy's internal fraud rules, investigation procedures, thresholds, systems, or customer data.

---

## Investigation Approach

A strong validation investigation should consider the complete evidence available rather than relying on a single risk indicator.

My proposed approach is:

**Identify → Investigate → Validate → Decide → Communicate → Escalate**

### 1. Identify

Determine what triggered the review.

Examples include:

* Identity verification failure
* Name mismatch
* Multiple applications
* Unusual device activity
* Phone-related indicators
* Document-quality concerns
* Recent SIM changes

### 2. Investigate

Review the available information surrounding the indicator.

Questions may include:

* Are multiple indicators present?
* Do the identity details agree?
* Could the activity have a legitimate explanation?
* Is the issue isolated or part of a wider pattern?

### 3. Validate

Assess whether the available evidence supports proceeding, requesting additional verification, or escalating the case.

### 4. Decide

Possible outcomes include:

* Proceed with standard validation
* Request additional verification
* Refer for manual review
* Hold and investigate
* Escalate a potential system or operational issue

The decision should be based on the available evidence and applicable procedures.

### 5. Communicate

Where customer interaction is required:

* Listen to the customer's explanation.
* Communicate clearly and respectfully.
* Explain the next steps.
* Avoid revealing sensitive internal fraud-detection information.
* Avoid requesting unnecessary personal information.

### 6. Escalate

Cases should be escalated when:

* Evidence remains inconclusive.
* Identity concerns cannot be resolved.
* There are multiple significant indicators.
* A customer disputes a decision that requires further review.
* A repeated pattern suggests a possible system or operational issue.
