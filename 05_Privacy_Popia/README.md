# Privacy & POPIA

## Overview

This section demonstrates how privacy and data-protection principles can be incorporated into a fintech validation and fraud-prevention workflow in South Africa.

The approach is informed by principles associated with the **Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA)** and focuses on practical handling of customer information during validation, investigations and customer support.

This is an independent portfolio project using fictional scenarios.

---

## Objectives

The section demonstrates how I would approach:

* Protecting customer personal information
* Applying data-minimisation principles
* Limiting access to information based on role and necessity
* Handling identity and application information securely
* Protecting information belonging to other customers
* Maintaining accurate customer information
* Considering appropriate information retention
* Communicating with customers without exposing confidential fraud controls
* Reducing unnecessary privacy risks during investigations

---

## Privacy-by-Design Approach

The workflow is based on a simple principle:

**Access only what is necessary, use it responsibly, protect it appropriately, and avoid unnecessary exposure.**

This approach is particularly relevant when validation and fraud-prevention processes involve identity documents, contact information, application information and risk indicators.

## Validation & Customer Support Connection

Privacy should be considered throughout the validation process rather than only after a potential fraud case has been identified.

For example:

**Validation → Investigation → Decision → Customer Communication → Documentation**

At each stage, the representative should consider whether the information being accessed, recorded or disclosed is necessary and authorised.
